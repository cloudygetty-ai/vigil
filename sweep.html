Safety Sweep

A live check of the network you're on, then twelve checks that find what someone put on this phone — and shut the door they used. Everything here is built into iOS.

iOS locks every app inside its own sandbox. That protects you, but it also means nothing can list every file on your phone — not this page, not an App Store "scanner", not Apple. Anything claiming a full virus scan of an iPhone is showing you an animation.

What a payload can't hide is what it needs to work: a configuration profile, an app that skipped the App Store, a permission it was granted, or a device on your account. That's what these checks hunt.

1

Turn AirDrop receiving off

Now

If AirDrop is set to Everyone, anyone within about 30 feet can push files at you. This is the door most people leave open.

Settings › General › AirDrop › Receiving Off
Set toReceiving Off, or Contacts Only if you use it.
Bad"Everyone". Note that "Everyone for 10 Minutes" expires by itself — if it shows Everyone permanently, something set it that way.
2

Look for a configuration profile

Now

The most important check here. A configuration profile is a small file that can be AirDropped or emailed to you. One tap installs it, and it can add a VPN, reroute your traffic, install a certificate that lets someone read your encrypted browsing, and lock settings you can't change back. It's the most powerful thing anyone can put on an iPhone without jailbreaking it.

Settings › General › VPN & Device Management
CleanThe row doesn't exist at all, or it opens to Not Connected with nothing listed. No row under General is the best outcome.
BadAny profile you didn't install. Tap it → Remove Profile. If it won't remove, or wants a passcode you never set, go to the last section.
3

Check what's signed into your account

Now

Someone else's device on your Apple Account is far worse than anything AirDropped — it can mean your photos, messages, backups and location are readable continuously, from anywhere.

Settings › [your name at the top] › scroll to the device list
CleanEvery device listed is one you own and could point at right now.
BadAnything unfamiliar. Tap it → Remove from Account, then change your Apple Account password and turn on two-factor.
02

Find what's there

Sweep for anything that got in and stayed.

4

Run Apple's Safety Check

Check

Apple built this for exactly your situation. It shows every person and app you're sharing data with, in one place, and can cut it all off at once. Needs iOS 16 or newer.

Settings › Privacy & Security › Safety Check
UseManage Sharing & Access to review calmly — people, then apps, then account security.
UrgentEmergency Reset stops all sharing with everyone immediately. Use it if you think access is ongoing.
5

Read your full app list

Check

The Home Screen hides apps in folders, in the App Library, and on pages you've switched off. This screen shows everything installed, no exceptions.

Settings › General › iPhone Storage
CleanYou recognise every entry. Scroll the whole list — don't skim the bottom.
BadAnything unfamiliar, or with a generic name and blank icon. Tap → Delete App.
6

Audit microphone, camera and location

Check

Work through these one at a time. Each screen lists every app that asked, and whether you granted it.

Settings › Privacy & Security › Microphone / Camera / Location / Photos / Contacts
CleanEvery app with access has an obvious reason. Location on While Using rather than Always.
BadAnything on Always location, or mic/camera access that makes no sense. Switch it off — apps just ask again.
7

Check for a keyboard with Full Access

Check

A third-party keyboard granted "Full Access" can see every character you type — passwords, messages, card numbers — and send it anywhere. Quiet, effective, and almost nobody looks here.

Settings › General › Keyboard › Keyboards
CleanOnly English (or your languages) and Emoji. Those are Apple's own.
BadAny keyboard you didn't add. Swipe left to delete. If you keep one, turn Allow Full Access off.
8

Check location sharing and Screen Time

Check

Two settings meant for families that get quietly repurposed to monitor adults. Screen Time can report activity elsewhere and be locked with a passcode you don't know.

Settings › [your name] › Find My › Share My Location Settings › Screen Time
CleanSharing location only with people you chose. Screen Time off, or with no passcode you didn't set.
BadAnyone unexpected under Share My Location — tap → Stop Sharing. Or a Screen Time passcode you never created.
03

Close it behind you

Revoke old access and make the next attempt harder.

9

Revoke every trusted computer

Harden

Every time you tapped "Trust This Computer", iOS saved a permanent pairing for that machine. It survives reboots and lets that computer pull a full backup whenever connected. No screen lists them — this clears them all.

Settings › General › Transfer or Reset iPhone › Reset › Reset Location & Privacy
SafeDoes not erase your data. Clears pairings and resets app permissions, so apps ask again. Worth doing regardless.
WhyIf anyone ever plugged your phone into their computer — even "just to charge" — this is the only way to take that access back.
10

Set a strong passcode

Harden

Everything above assumes nobody can just pick your phone up and unlock it. Four digits is read over your shoulder in one glance; six is a thousand times harder.

Settings › Face ID & Passcode › Change Passcode › Passcode Options
Set toCustom Numeric Code, six digits or more. Not a birthday, not a year.
AlsoOn that screen turn off Accessories under "Allow Access When Locked" — that blocks USB access to a locked phone.
11

Update iOS

Harden

Most real iPhone attacks use a flaw Apple already fixed. Boring, and one of the highest-value things on this list.

Settings › General › Software Update
DoInstall anything pending, then turn on Automatic Updates and Security Responses.
BadSeveral versions behind. If the phone can't update at all, it's no longer getting security fixes.
12

Consider Lockdown Mode

Harden

Apple's maximum-security setting. Blocks incoming invitations and attachment types from anyone you haven't messaged first, and shuts off wired connections to a locked phone.

Settings › Privacy & Security › Lockdown Mode
ForAnyone who believes a specific person is targeting them. A real, well-built feature — not a gimmick.
CostSome sites, attachments and features stop working. A deliberate trade. Turn it off any time.

If you're still not satisfied

There's an honest limit to what any check can prove. These find what a payload needs to work — but they can't prove the absence of something, and a file saved quietly inside another app's storage is invisible to every tool that exists.

If a profile wouldn't remove, if there's a Screen Time passcode you didn't set, or you simply can't shake it, one move definitively works and needs no computer:

Settings › General › Transfer or Reset iPhone › Erase All Content and Settings. Back up to iCloud first. That wipes everything an attacker could have left, at every level a non-jailbroken iPhone can be reached.

One caution: restoring a backup can carry a bad app or profile straight back. If you erase because you found something real, set up as new and reinstall apps by hand. A tedious evening, and completely conclusive.

If the person you're worried about is close to you, Apple's Safety Check (check 4) was designed for exactly that, and its Emergency Reset cuts every form of sharing at once.

Every step uses settings built into iOS. The network check runs from this phone and reads nothing about you beyond what any website already sees. Progress ticks are saved only in this browser.
